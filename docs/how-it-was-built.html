Chrysopoeia

The making of a small model that speaks in an esoteric register by default — what worked, what didn’t, and the one idea it turned on.

SmolLM3‑3B· huggingface / chrysopoeia · source

What it is. SmolLM3-3B-Base, soaked in public-domain Western-esoteric prose and given a light turn-taking pass, so it answers any question — a dripping faucet, a job interview, the nature of the divine light — in the voice of the early-20th-century occult revival (Hall, Lévi, the Kybalion), with no retrieval at inference.

It is an exploration, not a product, and not an oracle: it grades on the quality of the voice, not doctrinal accuracy, and will fabricate esoterica fluently. Don’t trust it for facts.

The bet

Can a disposition live in the weights?

Three things a model can carry: knowledge (what texts say), voice (the register), behavior (how to respond). The hypothesis was that you could bake an esoteric disposition into the weights so the register is the model’s default distribution — not a trick it does when the input looks mystical, but the dialect it always speaks. Knowledge-as-recall was deliberately not a goal.

The plan was two phases: soak the base on esoteric prose until the register is native, then a light, register-neutral SFT for turn-taking — betting the response comes out in-register because the esoteric voice is the dialect the soaked base completes in most fluently. That bet turned out to be half right, and finding out which half is the story.

☼
I the first soak

It speaks — but only when cued

A 2.6M-token soak corpus, streamed straight out of the guru project’s Postgres dump and gated to confirmed public domain. A shallow QLoRA soak plus a 36-example neutral turn-taking SFT produced a model that spoke and took turns — and, on esoteric prompts, real in-register prose. But on mundane prompts it answered like a plain assistant: “check the battery,” “vegetable stir-fry.” The register was triggered by topic, not the default. Exactly the failure the design named as the thing to beat.

II soak harder

Knowledge deepens, disposition doesn’t

The obvious move: soak more — rank 128, higher learning rate, eight epochs, the whole trajectory snapshotted. Loss cratered to 0.11, stepping down at every epoch boundary: the adapter was memorizing the corpus, not shifting a distribution. And reading across the snapshots, mundane prompts stayed mundane at every depth. What deepened was esoteric content — by the deep snapshots the model reached for genuine Neoplatonic substance.

Why: a LoRA freezes the base and adds low-rank deltas — enough to add an esoteric mode, structurally weak at moving the unconditional output distribution. And 2.6M tokens against an 11-trillion-token base is a drop; pushing hard enough to move the global prior just memorizes.

III the mundane slice

The mechanism — then a collapse

Disposition had to come from somewhere else, and the design already named where: a mundane-input slice. The move that severs topic from register is training on ordinary questions answered in the register. Thirty-five hand-authored pairs on a mid soak snapshot, and it worked — the register became the default on mundane inputs, the answer still carried inside the voice:

My car won’t start. What should I check?

“A spark of life is the smallest fire… clean the terminals of the white salt of sulphur… the starter motor must be given its due.”

But on esoteric prompts it collapsed into oracular repetition loops. A decode penalty only traded loops for run-on drift. The loops were a narrow-cadence artifact of a tiny set — the fix wasn’t a knob, it was more, more diverse data.

IV the compiler

Grounding at scale

To scale the slice without a monoculture of one model’s guesses, we did what the design calls retrieval relocated to training time: reuse the live guru RAG as the compiler. Human-written prompts from Dolly-15k, filtered off the esoteric manifold so the register has to generalize; each grounded in real passages pulled from the running pgvector corpus; a local 27B writing a short in-register answer grounded in that real prose — correct guidance, clothed in the voice. Hundreds of grounded pairs, no product forked.

V it holds

Register as the default

Retrained on the same snapshot with the scaled slice, and no repetition penalty: the register is the default on mundane inputs and still useful, and the collapse is gone — coherent on esoteric prompts, reaching for real vocabulary that surfaced from the soak’s substance.

Tell me about the nature of the divine light

“The divine light is not a thing that illuminates from without… the source and the lamp, the light and the space it fills, all one act of a single power.”

Merged, exported to GGUF, it runs standalone in llama.cpp — no adapters, no retrieval — speaking in the esoteric register by default, even holding a metaphor across a two-turn chat despite single-turn training. That became the first release.

How’s the weather?

“The heavens are hung with clouds that move as the world moves… Know that weather is a small portent, like the birds that fly before a storm.”

The recipe

What actually works

SmolLM3-3B-Base
  → QLoRA soak on ~2.6M tokens of PD esoteric prose   [ SUBSTANCE ]
     (constant LR; use a MID snapshot — the final memorizes)
  → Phase-2 SFT on a scaled, RAG-grounded slice:       [ DISPOSITION ]
     { mundane input → in-register answer }  (+ esoteric Q&A)
  → merge adapters → GGUF → serve standalone

The one-line lesson: soak for what the register knows; SFT a grounded, topic-severed slice for the register itself. Soak depth alone will not get you there.

Provenance & honest limits

What’s baked in, and what to distrust

The soak corpus is confirmed US public-domain prose only — the revival shelf is all pre-1930 — so the published weights are copyright-clean by construction. The base is Apache-2.0; the slice prompts are Dolly-15k (CC-BY-SA).

By design it grades voice over correctness: it will state esoteric “facts” fluently whether faithful or not. The register in the slice is a 27B’s grounded approximation of the voice, not raw period prose — a real ceiling. And the reads here are by human eye; there is no automated grader yet.
